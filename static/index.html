Why pilots stall

The demo was
the easy part.

Gartner expects over 40% of agentic AI projects to be cancelled by the end of 2027, mostly over cost, unclear value and weak risk controls (source).

The model is rarely the problem. What's missing is the unglamorous part: tests, traces, budgets, permissions and a way back. AWS now ships most of those pieces inside AgentCore. Someone still has to set them up, prove them on your agent, and hand them to your team. That's the job.

Why your own account

Your account, your team,
your agent.

Everything runs in your AWS account, under your IAM, on AWS's own agent services rather than a tool I sell. Your team gets the dashboards, the eval suite, the policies and the runbook. When I finish, they can run it without me. That's my test of done.

What I won't do

  • I won't sell you a platform. AgentCore already has the parts; I configure them and prove they work on your agent.
  • I won't take a second engagement while yours is running.
  • I won't be your 24/7 pager. Your team owns on-call, and leaves the sprint with the alarms, runbook and rollback to do it.
  • I won't build anything only I can maintain. If your team can't run it, it isn't finished.
  • If your agent shouldn't go to production yet, the scorecard will say so, in writing.
  • If it's a five-minute fix, I'll tell you it's a five-minute fix, and I won't turn it into a project.
