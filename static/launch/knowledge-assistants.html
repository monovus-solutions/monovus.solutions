Give employees answers from your own documents that cite their sources, respect who may see what and cost a known amount per question.

The problem

What usually stops this kind of agent going live.

Answers without sources

People can't tell a right answer from a confident wrong one, so they stop using the assistant or, worse, trust it.

It shows people what they shouldn't see

If the index ignores document permissions, one HR or finance file in the wrong answer ends the project.

Last year's policy

Documents change and the index doesn't, so the assistant quotes rules that no longer apply.

Approach

Assess, harden, launch.

Three phases, typically 2 to 3 weeks for one agent, at a fixed fee agreed in writing before the first day.

Week 1

Assess

  • Score the assistant against the eight dimensions and this checklist
  • Collect 50 real employee questions with known answers and the documents that hold them
  • Map every document source, who may see it and how often it changes
  • Measure retrieval quality and cost per question today

Weeks 2 to 3

Harden

  • Access filters applied in the search, so answers use only documents the asker may see
  • Retrieval and answers tested separately, as a release gate
  • Source citations on every answer, and 'I don't know' tested as a correct answer
  • An index refresh schedule and a tested way to remove a document

Final week

Launch

  • Rollout to one team first, then wider
  • A weekly list of unanswered questions, to find gaps in your documentation
  • Runbook, evidence pack and before-and-after scorecard
  • Handover to the team that owns the knowledge

The checklist

The eight dimensions, applied to knowledge assistants.

The checks we work through on every engagement of this kind. Use them on your own agent: each unticked line is a risk someone should own.

V · value

A clear owner and a measurable payoff

  • An owner in the function that owns the knowledge, such as HR, IT or operations
  • One target, such as help-desk tickets avoided or hours saved each week, against a baseline
  • Usage measured weekly, so you know whether people trust it

C1 · risk

Quality you can measure

  • Real employee questions with known answers and the documents that hold them
  • Retrieval tested separately from answering: did it find the right passage?
  • 'I don't know' tested as the right answer when the documents don't cover the question

C2 · risk

Every decision visible

  • Every answer traced to the passages it used
  • A list of questions the assistant couldn't answer, reviewed weekly
  • Alarms on empty or failed searches

C3 · cost

Predictable cost

  • Cost per question, and the cost of keeping the index current
  • Limits on how much text is sent to the model per question
  • Budgets with alarms, and simple questions on a smaller model

C4 · risk

The agent can only do what it should

  • Search results filtered by the asker's permissions before the model sees them
  • Confidential collections left out until their access rules are mapped
  • Read-only by default: no changes in other systems without approval

C5 · risk

Access you can account for

  • The assistant knows who is asking, through your existing sign-in
  • Connector credentials in a secrets manager, rotated
  • No shared administrator account reading every document store

C6 · risk

A fast way back

  • Index, prompts and settings versioned
  • Written, tested steps to remove a document from answers quickly
  • A rollback rehearsed before launch

C7 · risk

Ready for incidents and audits

  • A runbook for a document shown to the wrong people: remove it, find the affected answers, tell the owner
  • Named owners for each document source
  • An evidence pack: sources indexed, who can see what and how it was tested

Questions

About knowledge assistants.

Which document sources can it use?

Amazon Bedrock Knowledge Bases reads documents from Amazon S3 and, through connectors, from several common document systems. We map your sources in the first week and say plainly which ones need extra work.

Will it respect who can see what?

Yes, and that is a launch condition. Documents carry access metadata, and searches are filtered by the asker's permissions before the model sees anything. Collections whose permissions can't be mapped stay out.

How do we stop it making things up?

It answers from retrieved passages, cites them, and is tested on saying 'I don't know'. An answer without support in the documents is counted as a defect.

What happens when a document changes?

The index refreshes on a schedule we agree with you, and there are written, tested steps for removing a document from answers quickly.

What does each question cost?

It depends on the model, how much text is retrieved and the volume. We measure it in the first week and set budgets before rollout.

Do we have to use Amazon Bedrock Knowledge Bases?

It is our default because it keeps the index in your AWS account. If you already run your own search index on AWS, we can keep it and add the same controls around it.
