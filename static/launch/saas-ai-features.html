Monovus Launch for SaaS AI Features

Ship agent features that pass your customers' security reviews, keep every tenant's data separate and have a known cost per customer.

Take the free assessment Talk to us

The problem

What usually stops this kind of agent going live.

Security reviews stall deals

Enterprise buyers ask what the agent can reach, where their data goes and how you would know if it misbehaved.

Tenant boundaries

One customer's data in another customer's answer is the kind of incident that ends contracts.

Unknown margins

Without cost per tenant, pricing the feature is a guess, and heavy users can erase the margin.

Approach

Assess, harden, launch.

Three phases, typically 3 to 4 weeks for one agent, at a fixed fee agreed in writing before the first day.

Week 1

Assess

  • Score the feature against the eight dimensions and this checklist
  • Map every tool, data store and tenant boundary the agent touches
  • Measure cost per tenant and per request today
  • Collect the security questions your customers already ask

Weeks 2 to 3

Harden

  • Tenant scope enforced by policy on every tool call
  • Evaluations in your CI, gating releases and model upgrades
  • Traces and cost tagged by tenant, with per-tenant limits
  • The user's identity and per-tenant credentials carried through

Final week

Launch

  • Feature-flagged rollout, tenant by tenant
  • Security questionnaire answers and an architecture diagram for your sales team
  • Runbook, evidence pack and before-and-after scorecard
  • Handover to your product and platform teams

The checklist

The eight dimensions, applied to SaaS AI features.

The checks we work through on every engagement of this kind. Use them on your own agent: each unticked line is a risk someone should own.

V · value

A clear owner and a measurable payoff

  • A product owner and one metric, such as adoption or retention, set against cost to serve
  • Cost per tenant used in pricing and packaging
  • A rule for which plans get which models and limits

C1 · risk

Quality you can measure

  • Evaluations run in CI on every release and model upgrade, and block regressions
  • Tests for each customer configuration you support
  • Cross-tenant tests: no answer may use another tenant's data

C2 · risk

Every decision visible

  • Traces tagged by tenant and user
  • Your support team can see what the agent did for a given customer
  • Alarms per tenant on errors and unusual usage

C3 · cost

Predictable cost

  • Cost per tenant and per feature, reported monthly
  • Per-tenant limits and quotas
  • Budgets with alarms, and model choice tied to the customer's plan

C4 · risk

The agent can only do what it should

  • Every tool call scoped to the caller's tenant by policy, outside the prompt
  • Customer admins decide which actions the agent may take in their account
  • No search index or memory shared across tenants

C5 · risk

Access you can account for

  • The agent acts with the end user's identity and tenant
  • Per-tenant integration credentials in a secrets manager, rotated
  • No shared superuser account behind the feature

C6 · risk

A fast way back

  • Prompts, models and settings versioned with each release
  • Feature flags to roll back for one tenant or for everyone
  • Staged rollout, starting with a small group of tenants

C7 · risk

Ready for incidents and audits

  • A runbook, including how you tell customers about an AI incident
  • Security questionnaire answers and a current architecture diagram
  • An evidence pack updated with every release

Questions

About SaaS AI features.

Will this slow our release cycle?

The evaluation suite runs in your CI like any other test. It adds minutes to a release and takes the guesswork out of model upgrades.

How do you prove tenant isolation?

Tenant scope is enforced by policy on every tool call and tested with cross-tenant cases in the evaluation suite. The results go into the evidence pack.

Can different plans use different models?

Yes. Model choice and limits can follow the customer's plan, which is often the simplest way to protect margin.

Do you work with our developers, or hand over at the end?

With them, from the first day. Everything lands in your repositories and CI, and your team runs it afterwards.

What do we give our customers' security teams?

An architecture diagram, answers to the usual AI security questions and the evidence pack for the release they are buying.

Our product isn't on AWS yet.

Then we are probably the wrong choice for now. We build only on AWS, in your account.
