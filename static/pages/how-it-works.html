How it works

Two weeks,
one agent.

The sprint is ten working days on a single agent that already works in a demo. It ends with your agent measured, guarded and handed to your team, and a written list of anything still standing between it and production.

Before

The free review

45 minutes on a call. You show me the agent; I score it against the seven controls. If a sprint makes sense, you get a fixed quote and a written scope within two working days.

Days 1–2

Baseline

Access through an IAM role you create and can revoke. I map the agent's tools, data and permissions, switch on tracing, and score the seven controls as they stand.

Days 3–5

Evals

With your team I build a golden set of at least 25 real cases, run it, and wire it into your release process so a worse score blocks the release.

Days 6–8

Guardrails

Policy on tool access, a least-privilege role, identity for the agent's actions, cost budgets with alarms, and model routing where a smaller model will do.

Days 9–10

The way back, and handover

A tested rollback, a runbook, the before-and-after scorecard, and a ranked backlog of what's left. We finish with a handover session for whoever will own the agent.

What you keep

  • An eval suiteThe golden set and the release gate, in your repository.
  • Dashboards and alarmsTraces, quality and cost, in your CloudWatch.
  • Policies and rolesAs code, reviewed with your team.
  • A runbookWhat to do when it misbehaves, and how to roll back.
  • Two scorecardsBefore and after, against the same seven controls.
  • A backlogWhat's left, ranked by risk, with rough effort.

What I need from you

  • One named owner for the agent on your side.
  • About two hours a week of an engineer who knows the agent.
  • Real example conversations or tasks, to build the golden set from.
  • One AWS account to work in, through a role you control.
